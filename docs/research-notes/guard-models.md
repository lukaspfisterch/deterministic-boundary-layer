# Guard Models

Status: `hypothesis`

A strong recurring lesson from downstream research is that a promising family is not enough.

Before anything becomes policy-relevant, it needs an explicit guard model:

- what qualifies as entry
- what invalidates the state
- what ends the window
- what happens under delay or latency

Why this matters:

- it forces structure into a measurable form
- it keeps research observer-only instead of pretending that a good-looking family is already executable authority
- it reveals whether a family survives explicit guard assumptions or collapses immediately

Public takeaway:

the right next step after a promising family is usually not another label.
It is a narrower explicit model.
